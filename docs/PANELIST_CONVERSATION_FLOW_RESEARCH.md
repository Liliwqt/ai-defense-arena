# Natural panelist reactions and research-defense conversation

Research date: **2026-10-11**. Scope: brief acknowledgments, selective encouragement and tactful concern in research defenses. Research and design guidance only; no implementation or live quality review. It extends [the earlier questioning research](PANELIST_QUESTIONING_RESEARCH.md).

**Recommendation:** make the panelist responsive to the meaning of the actual answer. A short “Oh, I see” can acknowledge a newly understood reason; praise should identify something explained well; concern should identify a confirmed limitation and its consequence. Let a sufficient answer close an issue. Human conversation needs judgment about what happens next, alongside natural wording.

**Subsequent implementation, 2026-10-11:** the user requested the refinement.
The shared conversation prompt now explicitly allows optional acknowledgments,
specific encouragement and proportionate concern, and describes answer-guided
clarification/probe/advance decisions. It retains role voices, language matching,
existing request counts, citations, timers and response bounds. The research and
illustrations below remain design evidence; they are not live AI output or
prewritten defense scripts. Offline verification is recorded in PROJECT_LOG.md;
live conversation quality and user review remain pending.

## What the primary sources establish

**Institutional recommendations:** expectations within each institution, not observations of all panelists or a universal Philippine/UK/US protocol.

- **Westminster, research degrees, August 2025:** questions should arise from the submission. Its good-practice appendix emphasizes asking, fairness, clarifying confusion and candidate well-being during a stressful examination. It describes the viva as a formal discussion and cautions against implying an outcome in advance. This supports calm, grounded reactions without announcing that the student has passed. [Examiner guidance, Appendix 2, PDF p. 19](https://www.westminster.ac.uk/sites/default/public-files/general-documents/Guidance-for-Examiners-of-Research-Degrees_0.pdf#page=19).
- **Southampton, postgraduate research, February 2019:** constructive questions examine why a method was chosen instead of assuming an examiner's alternative would have prevented problems. Candidates may need thinking time; an incisive or interesting answer can deserve acknowledgment; rephrasing can help a candidate recover from misunderstanding or nervousness. Hostile rapid questioning is identified as poor practice. This is a recommendation about examination conduct, not experimental proof that a particular acknowledgment reduces anxiety. [Examiner guidance, PDF pp. 5–6](https://cdn.southampton.ac.uk/assets/imported/transforms/content-block/UsefulDownloads_Download/D22673A2785F4491898FCEE0B1394F1D/Guidance%20for%20Examiners%20of%20PGR%20Awards.pdf#page=5).
- **Harper Adams, undergraduate viva:** tailor questions to the student, progress logically, allow the student's voice to be heard, rephrase when they struggle and move on when they cannot answer. Its challenge level and assessment arrangements are specifically undergraduate. Its adaptable discussion ideas should not become compulsory lines or timings in this app. [Staff guidance, PDF pp. 7–8](https://cdn.harper-adams.ac.uk/document/page/155_Staff-Guidance-for-Conducting-an-Undergraduate-Viv.pdf#page=7).
- **LPU-Batangas, Philippine thesis/dissertation manual, 2023:** examiners give constructive comments and suggestions. Proposal responsibilities concern significance, relevance, contribution and objectives; final-defense responsibilities examine whether reported results answer the approved objectives and whether conclusions and recommendations fit. This supports stage-sensitive discussion rather than treating missing proposal results as a failure. [Responsibilities of examiners, PDF p. 18](https://research.lpubatangas.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Manual-for-Thesis-and-Dissertation-2023.pdf#page=18).
- **Georgia Southern, Educational Leadership doctorate:** questions may examine past work or future possibilities and need not all have predetermined answers. Give the candidate room to explain; avoid lengthy examiner presentations and directing their method. Relevant questioning precedes suggestions when understanding still needs development. [Defense guidelines, “Questioning Process/Protocol”](https://ww2.georgiasouthern.edu/coe/edld/dissertation/defense-guidelines/).

**Empirical conversation research:** these studies analyze interactions outside this simulator. Their findings inform distinctions, not a validated defense-dialogue recipe.

- **Heritage:** analysis of naturally occurring conversation identifies “oh” as displaying a change in the recipient's knowledge or awareness. Its position and accompanying words matter; it is not inherently praise or agreement. [Original chapter, publisher record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/structures-of-social-action/changeofstate-token-and-aspects-of-its-sequential-placement/8AACF1699E4A7AE1B19D8248B6613405); [author-uploaded original text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/345229295_A_change-of-state_token_and_aspects_of_its_sequential_placement).
- **Gardner, 1998:** listener responses convey how talk has been received and affect its trajectory. The study examines “yeah,” “mm hm” and “mm” through their placement, prosody and surrounding pauses. It supports distinguishing meaningful acknowledgment from indiscriminate filler, but does not establish that adding written “hmmm” makes an AI examiner natural. [Original study, author-uploaded text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/31025408_Between_Speaking_and_Listening_The_Vocalisation_of_Understandings1).
- **Lindström and colleagues, 2019:** in 426 Swedish-language service encounters, positive expressions including “perfect” help complete tasks and segment the interaction. These are box-office exchanges in Sweden and Finland, not research defenses or evidence about English/Cebuano examiner preferences. [Original article](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08351813.2019.1581468).
- **West, 2020:** analysis of one undergraduate supervision meeting shows advice negotiated and adjusted in response to the student's resistance. This supports listening to a student's reasons before repeating advice; a single supervision encounter cannot establish a general defense rule. [Original article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42438-020-00194-5).

## How to interpret the requested expressions

The following is **design inference**, informed by those distinctions. Expressions are optional resources, not a phrase bank to cycle through.

| Expression | Useful conversational purpose | Judgment needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Oh, I see.” | Receive a newly explained reason or correct an earlier understanding. | Show what became clearer when needed. Understanding the explanation does not verify the claim. |
| “Hmm, noted.” | Briefly register a consequential detail before considering it. | Bare “noted” can feel dismissive in text. Explain a real unresolved point rather than performing thoughtfulness with repeated sounds. |
| “Perfect.” | Occasionally close a small, clearly satisfied conversational task. | It can imply a strong evaluation. Prefer specific recognition such as “That explains the sampling choice” when the study remains open to challenge. |
| “Oh no! So you don't have that?” | The intended function appears to be surprise or concern about a gap. | An emphatic alarm can sound humiliating. First establish whether the gap exists; then describe its consequence calmly and ask one focused question. |

Prefer ordinary spelling: stretched “oww” or “seee” may be playful or ambiguous in formal text and needs user review. A bare acknowledgment can work when its meaning is clear. Avoid acknowledgment quotas, compulsory praise and negative reactions to every limitation.

Absence from an extracted paper, a short answer or a timeout is not confirmation that a safeguard or analysis does not exist. Distinguish “not described here,” “not yet planned,” “not done” and “done but not reported.” Respond to the state actually established.

## A coherent conversation flow

This proposed flow synthesizes the evidence; it is not an institutional procedure:

**Read the source → ask one focused question → listen → choose an appropriate response → clarify, probe or move on → offer occasional conditional advice → close with grounded coaching.**

1. Start from a cited choice or claim and the study's stage. No fabricated opening reaction to an answer that has not happened.
2. Receive the answer before deciding whether acknowledgment, recognition, concern or silence helps. React to its reason, evidence or constraint rather than repeating its topic.
3. If the defender misunderstands, explain the same question's intent. If one consequential detail remains unresolved, ask a focused probe. If the explanation is sufficient, accept it and advance. An examiner's different preference alone does not justify another challenge.
4. When paper and answer appear inconsistent, state the apparent mismatch tentatively and allow correction. Update the conversation when the defender resolves it.
5. When advice is useful, tie one possible alternative to the established limitation, its benefit and a condition or cost. Preserve room for a justified decision to retain the method. Larger advice belongs in closing coaching.
6. Close by distinguishing explanations supported by actual exchanges from unresolved issues and proposed improvements. Do not turn examiner suggestions into student accomplishments.

Role manners can follow existing specialties: Methodology patiently traces a research decision; Ethics tactfully examines participant experience; Impact explores a realistic benefit; Critical tests a remaining assumption and accepts a sound explanation. These are design choices, not empirical personality types.

## Fit with the current app

Static inspection of [question_generator.py](../backend/question_generator.py) shows substantial support already: answer-specific reactions, sufficient-answer acceptance, distinct role voices, optional conditional advice and stage/language guidance. `CONVERSATION_GUIDANCE` rejects automatic praise and fake filler. A future refinement could distinguish useful discourse markers from empty performance while retaining those constraints; this note makes no edit.

The existing `lead_in` is a statement of zero to two short sentences, at most 300 characters; the one main question belongs in `question`. Therefore “Oh no! So you don't have that?” should not become an extra question in the reaction. Structural validation checks length/sentence limits; it does not guarantee thoughtful concern, grounding or naturalness.

The next asking panelist owns the generated reaction, potentially after a role change. [timed_turn.py](../backend/timed_turn.py) uses a 4–12-second speaking phase for nonempty reactions before the next question and full 15-second vote; empty reactions skip it. These timings are app behavior, not research findings.

Existing clarification preserves intent/question/citation; [defense_session.py](../backend/defense_session.py) retains the selected speaker and question number for one optional probe's 30-second reply; a new follow-up creates a turn with voting. A probe already allows acknowledgment within its 300-character request. Transcripts preserve exchanges. Completion produces no extra dialogue; coaching closes. A final standalone acknowledgment would be a separate design decision, not an existing capability or research requirement.

## Original illustrative exchanges

Hypothetical examples only, not quotations, observations or lines to install in code. Live questions require uploaded citations.

**Adequate explanation and transition**

> **Methodology:** Why recruit first-year students for this study?
> **Team:** Our objective is specifically their transition into university, so we excluded other years.
> **Reaction:** Oh, I see. That explains the boundary of your sample.
> **Next question:** How will you reach first-year students outside your own department?

**Confirmed proposal gap and conditional suggestion**

> **Team:** We haven't planned how to check the interview guide yet.
> **Probe:** What would you need to check before using it with participants?
> **Team:** Whether they understand the wording, but access is limited.
> **Later reaction/advice:** That leaves the wording untested. If access allows, a small pilot could help identify confusing items before recruitment.

**Apparent contradiction, then correction**

> **Panelist:** The paper describes random sampling, but you mentioned volunteers. How were participants actually selected?
> **Team:** Volunteers joined the pool; we randomly selected from that pool.
> **Reaction:** Thanks, that clarifies the two steps.

**Clarification preserves intent**

> **Team:** What do you mean by a limitation of the sample?
> **Panelist:** I mean which people your participants may not represent. I'm asking how that affects the scope of your conclusion.

**Timeout**

> **Panelist reaction:** We didn't receive an answer in that turn.

No inferred nervousness, incompetence or missing method.

**Proposal versus final**

> **Proposal:** How will you check whether participants understand the consent form?
> **Final:** What evidence shows participants understood the consent form?

Follow the established language preference, including existing conversational Cebuano/English guidance. No native-speaker validation or evidence of equivalent Cebuano marker effects was established; native review remains necessary.

## Observable review criteria

For a later live synthetic defense, review the complete transcript and displayed transitions, including an adequate answer, uncertainty, correction, clarification, probe reply and timeout. Record whether:

- each reaction fits what was actually said; acknowledgment avoids false agreement and praise identifies a concrete merit;
- confirmed gaps receive proportionate concern, while document omissions and silence remain uncertain;
- questions are understandable and focused, clarified intent stays stable and resolved issues stop recurring;
- proposal plans, completed findings, hypothetical conditions and optional advice stay distinct;
- the next role connects naturally where useful, wording varies and reactions do not become mandatory prefixes;
- transcript attribution, language continuity and the room's actual phases remain coherent.

These are proposed qualitative criteria, not a score or tested benchmark. Official-source browsing and static source inspection support this note. No AI calls, runtime changes, live defense, hosted check, physical-device check or user review were performed. Implementation planning and live human/native-speaker review remain later work.
