# Synthetic interdisciplinary proposal: campus queue information and student experience
This document is synthetic and prepared only for local AI conversation review.
## Aim and research setting
The proposal combines human-computer interaction, education service experience and software design in one campus. It explores how students understand queue-status information and how a prototype represents that information.
## Research questions
What difficulties do students describe when deciding whether to visit the service office? Which interface information do they find useful or confusing? How can a prototype represent approximate queue status without pretending it measures exact waiting time?
## Qualitative component
The team plans semi-structured interviews with twelve student volunteers from at least three departments. Interview notes would be coded into themes; the team would retain contradictory comments and document revisions to its coding decisions. Twelve is an initial feasibility target rather than a saturation guarantee.
## Design component
A prototype displays a manually entered count and the time it was last updated. The accompanying code stores a queue count and update time. The count is a user-supplied estimate, not a verified observation of service duration or actual queue membership.
## Prototype evaluation
Participants would interpret mock status cards during short usability tasks. The team would record confusion and interpretation choices descriptively. No randomized trial, statistical hypothesis test, quantitative population estimate or real waiting-time reduction is specified in this proposal.
## Integration
Interview themes would guide wording and freshness indicators in the prototype. Usability observations would help refine the design. Multiple components do not by themselves establish a formally justified mixed-methods study.
## Safeguards
Participation is voluntary and unrelated to academic grades or office access. Notes would use participant codes. The prototype would contain no student identifiers, and the team would explain that an old update may be misleading. Device screenshots would contain synthetic counts only.
## Expected contribution
The intended contribution is a documented set of student information needs and design tradeoffs. The proposal does not claim a production deployment, a causal reduction in queue waits or broad generalization across campuses.
## Remaining gaps
The team still needs to justify its recruitment approach, specify how disagreements in coding are discussed, and determine when status should be marked stale. The current prototype accepts negative counts, which is an implementation limitation to be addressed before use.
