# Synthetic theoretical proposal: fair waiting as a procedural claim
This document is synthetic and prepared only for local AI conversation review.
## Aim and field
This conceptual paper examines procedural fairness in service queues using social philosophy and decision theory. It proposes an argument, not an empirical experiment or an already proven theorem.
## Central question
Under what assumptions does first-arrival service order count as procedurally fair, and when do justified exceptions undermine that claim?
## Definitions
Arrival means entry to a publicly observable queue rather than entry to the building. Procedural fairness here means that a publicly stated rule applies equally to cases considered relevantly alike. Equal moral worth is distinguished from identical practical needs.
## Working argument
First-arrival order can protect predictability and discourage favoritism when arrival is observable, participants can access the queue and exceptional priority rules are publicly justified. These are conditional assumptions, not facts demonstrated about every institution.
## Method and sources
The proposal uses conceptual analysis, reconstructs arguments from selected philosophical sources and tests its definitions with counterexamples. It does not recruit participants, collect interviews, conduct statistical tests or claim to estimate the public's attitudes.
## Counterexamples
A person with an urgent accessibility need may deserve priority even when they arrived later. A person whose arrival is unobservable challenges whether the rule can be applied consistently. These examples test the scope of the proposed definition without proving a practical policy is optimal.
## Integrity and ethical relevance
The relevant integrity tasks are accurate attribution, charitable treatment of opposing arguments and disclosure of value assumptions. The paper does not use personal datasets. An eventual policy discussion would need separate attention to exclusion and accessibility.
## Expected contribution
The intended contribution is a clarified distinction between predictability, equal treatment and justified priority. No commercial outcome or immediate change in service performance is claimed.
## Limitations and remaining work
The author has not yet established that the proposed assumptions are sufficient for fairness. The argument may depend on how relevant similarity is defined. The final paper will compare alternative priority rules and make its normative premises explicit.
