# Synthetic completed study: reading campus queue status
This document is synthetic and prepared only for local AI conversation review.
## Aim and setting
The study examined whether a queue-status prototype changed how quickly student volunteers could interpret a campus service status display in a classroom task. The study does not measure actual time spent in a live service queue.
## Research question
How do task completion times and interpretation errors differ between a conventional status notice and the prototype in this pilot?
## Design and recruitment
Twelve adult student volunteers from one department each completed both versions. Six saw the conventional notice first and six saw the prototype first. Recruitment was voluntary through the department announcement board; the sample was not randomly drawn from the entire university.
## Measures
Task completion time ran from displaying the notice to the volunteer identifying both the queue status and its last update time. An interpretation error meant either value was identified incorrectly. The task content and observer instructions were held constant across versions.
## Analysis and results
Median task time was 72 seconds for the conventional notice and 58 seconds for the prototype. Four of twelve volunteers made an interpretation error with the conventional notice; two of twelve made an error with the prototype. The pilot reports descriptive paired observations and no significance test or population effect estimate.
## Ethics and data handling
Volunteers provided informed consent and could withdraw without losing access to the service. Timing sheets used participant codes, not student names. No service records or sensitive personal data were collected. The team plans to delete the raw timing sheets after the documented retention period of six months.
## Contribution
The study identifies design questions about how users interpret freshness indicators. It contributes preliminary observations for refining interface guidance, not evidence of shorter real queues or a universal improvement.
## Limitations
The single-department volunteer sample, small sample size and classroom setting limit transfer to real queue behavior. Familiarity between tasks may affect the second attempt despite counterbalanced order. Further evaluation would need broader recruitment and real service conditions.
